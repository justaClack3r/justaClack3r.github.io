Context

Dielectric elastomer actuators (DEAs) are soft "artificial muscles": a thin elastomer film sandwiched between compliant electrodes. Apply a high voltage and the electrostatic pressure squeezes the film thinner, so it expands in area. The Harvard Microrobotics Lab uses DEAs to power insect-scale robots. Because the electrodes have to stretch with the film, they're made from thin carbon-based conductive layers rather than metal.

This gantry automates spraying a liquid carbon electrode material onto DEA films. Automating the spraying makes the coating repeatable, and enclosing the gantry keeps the aerosol contained.
