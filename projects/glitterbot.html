GlitterBot: ES 51 Best CAD

A competition robot for ES 51, Harvard's introductory engineering design course. I designed it in SolidWorks, our five-person team built and ran it, and it won the class's Best CAD award.

The challenge

The ES 51 competition, "Turf Wars", called for a robot that fits in an 11 × 11 × 11 in box, climbs 15° and 30° ramps, and picks up a puck, a block and a dog toy to place on a tic-tac-toe grid. We built the design around speed.

Design

  • All-wheel drive from two motors. Two DC gearmotors drive the rear wheels, and timing belts carry the drive to the front wheels for traction on the ramps.
  • Extending claw. The claw reaches out and up through a linkage that works like a steam-engine connecting rod, giving long reach from a compact package. Its four-bar gripper opens to 4 in, enough for the 3 in dog toy with room to spare.
  • Low center of gravity. With the claw retracted the robot stands just 7 in tall, while the other teams' claws sat at a fixed 10 in. The lower CG makes it much harder to tip on the 30° ramp.
  • Fabrication. Laser-cut acrylic plates, nylon gears broached for hex shafts, PLA motor mounts, and wheels cast in EcoFlex 50 silicone.

What I learned

In competition the claw worked every time. The drivetrain didn't: a gear mesh slipped under load and a tooth broke. The lesson was to give the unglamorous systems the same attention as the showpiece. That means retaining gears axially and testing the drivetrain as hard as the mechanism everyone's watching.
